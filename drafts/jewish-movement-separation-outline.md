# How Yeshua's Jewish Movement Became Separated from Judaism

**Status:** Editorial research outline — not approved for publication.

## Purpose

Investigate the historical processes by which the movement of Jewish disciples of Yeshua, originating within the Jewish world of the first century, came to be associated with increasingly separate Gentile Christian institutions. The study must distinguish contemporary evidence from later accounts and avoid presenting a complex process as one event or one centrally coordinated policy.

## Proposed structure

### 1. A Jewish movement in the first century
Jerusalem, the apostles, the Temple, synagogue life, and the Jewish setting of the earliest disciples. Primary texts to examine: Acts 2, Acts 15, Acts 21, Galatians 2, and Josephus where relevant. Explain the distinct genres and evidentiary limits of these sources.

### 2. Jerusalem after AD 70
The destruction of the Temple, Roman administration, and changes within Jewish communities. Avoid asserting that the destruction immediately ended Jewish participation in the Yeshua movement.

### 3. The fiscus Judaicus
What the Roman tax was, whom it applied to, and how its enforcement changed under the Flavian emperors and Nerva. Examine Suetonius, *Domitian* 12; Cassius Dio where applicable; and Nerva's coin legend *fisci Iudaici calumnia sublata*. Do not assume the tax alone explains the separation or infer individual motives without evidence.

### 4. The Bar Kokhba revolt, AD 132–135
Evidence for the revolt, Roman suppression, and subsequent changes in Jerusalem and Judea. Evaluate later Christian claims about Jewish followers of Yeshua and the revolt with care, including questions of chronology and source reliability.

### 5. Gentile communities and leadership
Explore the diversity of second-century assemblies and the developing ways Christians described their relationship to Jewish law, identity, and Scripture. Relevant primary authors for critical examination include Ignatius of Antioch, Justin Martyr, and Melito of Sardis. Distinguish their claims from the practices of all believers.

### 6. Imperial Christianity and institutional separation
Trace developments in the fourth century, including Constantine's reign and the Council of Nicaea, without projecting later institutional structures backward onto the first century. Analyze the evidence for policies affecting Jewish-Christian relations.

### 7. What can the evidence establish?
A source-led synthesis that separates documented developments, plausible interpretations, and unresolved questions.
