# Yeshua Roots — Outreach & Mercy (Founder Review Draft)

**Status: proposed ministry direction; not yet an operational service or founder-approved public policy.**

## Purpose

Yeshua Roots exists to make Yeshua, the living Jewish Messiah, known through Scripture and through practical acts of love. We take seriously His teaching in Matthew 25:31–46, alongside James 1:27, Isaiah 58:6–10, and the Torah's commands to care for the stranger, widow, and orphan. Read Matthew 25 in its original context rather than flattening the identity of “the least of these my brothers.”

## People we intend to serve

- People experiencing homelessness and food insecurity.
- Survivors of abuse, with trauma-informed and confidential referral pathways.
- Fatherless children and other vulnerable children, through vetted safeguarding partners.
- Isolated elderly people, widows, and people stranded without support.
- Refugees, displaced people, and foreigners who need practical help navigating local services.

Assistance must be offered with dignity and without conditions of religious conversion, ministry membership, public testimony, or donation.

## Funding principles

**The ministry is not operated to generate personal profit.** Scripture studies, community membership, prayer, and aid are free to recipients. We will not beg for money, use manipulative appeals, or pressure anyone to give. Voluntary donations may cover reasonable documented operational costs and direct mercy work. No donation confers special access or priority. Publish clear records of how donated funds are used, while protecting beneficiary privacy. Do not launch a donation mechanism until legal, financial, and stewardship responsibilities have been reviewed.

## Launch sequence

1. **Founder review:** approve exact public mission wording and initial geographic scope.
2. **Local partnerships:** identify and verify food banks, shelters, social workers, child-protection specialists, refugee assistance providers, and elder-care partners.
3. **Safeguarding:** adopt procedures for minors, abuse survivors, confidential records, referrals, volunteer screening, and emergencies; never publish beneficiary stories without informed consent.
4. **Pilot food outreach:** begin with a small, accountable local food-distribution partnership; track meals and supplies delivered without collecting unnecessary personal data.
5. **Transparent stewardship:** establish expense approval, receipts, donation accounting, and periodic public impact reporting before soliciting or accepting gifts.
6. **Expand carefully:** add referrals and direct support for other groups only where trained volunteers and reliable partners can deliver safely.

## What is not live

No active food distribution, assistance intake, donation collection, volunteer enrollment, or outreach partnership is represented by this document. Do not publish contact forms promising urgent aid until staffing, response times, and escalation procedures are in place.

## Decisions for founder

- First city or region for a food outreach pilot.
- Whether to partner with an existing food bank or shelter before independent distributions.
- Who will own safeguarding, volunteer coordination, and financial oversight.
- Exact public mission wording and reporting commitments.
